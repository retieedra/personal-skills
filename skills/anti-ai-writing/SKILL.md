# Anti-AI Writing

Help the writer say something specific in language they would choose. Treat “anti-AI” as shorthand for resisting generic, interchangeable prose, not a promise to conceal assistance or pass a detector.

This is an evidence-informed editorial workflow, not a scientifically validated treatment. Research documents homogenization in particular writing settings; it does not establish a universal list of AI words or prove that these edits recover a person's original voice. Read [research notes](references/research.md) when explaining the evidence or making research claims.

## Establish what belongs to this writer

Use the request, draft, audience, and any genuine writing samples already provided. Resolve priorities in this order: explicit user instructions, intended meaning and evidence, audience and genre, then optional stylistic improvements.

Notice observable choices: degree of formality, directness, sentence connections, contractions, punctuation, recurring expressions, humor, and emotional restraint or intensity. Treat these as provisional editing clues, not demographic or personality diagnoses. Preserve deliberate dialect, regional spelling, culturally specific examples, and code-switching. Do not assume standard American English is the target.

Without samples, use the draft as the available evidence of voice. You cannot reconstruct a voice you have never observed. Ask for missing context only when it would materially change the revision; otherwise make the smallest useful edit. Do not make the user supply a biography for an ordinary rewrite.

When drafting from scratch, build from the user's own argument, details, and chosen examples. An independent outline can help retain their direction, but it is an editorial precaution, not a proven antidote to homogenization. Mark missing evidence or ask for it instead of inventing facts or memories.

## Diagnose before changing

Identify the actual friction: an unsupported generalization, padded opening, unclear subject, repeated conclusion, inflated certainty, mechanical transition, or wording that clashes with the surrounding voice. Fix that problem locally before rewriting a paragraph wholesale.

Keep a useful phrase even if it appears on an “AI words” list. Words such as “delve,” sentence fragments, parallel structures, and em dashes are not evidence of authorship. Honor a user's specific word preferences without converting them into universal rules.

Distinguish familiar language from empty language. A conventional phrase can suit a condolence note, technical explanation, or formal request. Remove it only when it adds no meaning, obscures the point, or weakens this writer's intention.

## Revise with restraint

- Replace abstract praise and sweeping claims with the supported subject, action, consequence, or example already available. If the draft supplies no specifics, narrow the claim or flag the gap; never fabricate numbers, citations, sensory detail, or lived experience.
- Cut duplicate setup, ceremonial conclusions, unnecessary intensifiers, and repeated paraphrases when they do no work. Keep repetition that provides emphasis, rhythm, or necessary clarification.
- Preserve qualifications such as “may,” “in this sample,” and “I think” when they express evidence limits or stance. Do not turn a tentative observation into a confident result to make it punchier.
- Let sentence shape follow the thought. Connect related ideas when that helps the reader, and use a short sentence when the point deserves space. Avoid prescribed sentence-length ratios, forced fragments, random synonyms, and punctuation quotas.
- Keep precise technical terms and consistent names. Variety is not a reason to replace the same concept with several near-synonyms. Formality, politeness, and complexity are legitimate choices when they serve the audience.
- Retain the writer's emotional temperature. Do not automatically make reserved prose enthusiastic, professional prose chatty, or an uneven personal account neatly inspirational.

Do not manufacture spelling mistakes, awkwardness, slang, or obscure vocabulary to signal humanity. Do not optimize against detector scores, assign an “AI percentage,” or imply a detector establishes who wrote a passage. If asked about detection, explain the limits briefly and offer normal editing based on meaning and voice.

## Check against the original

Before returning the revision, compare facts, attribution, certainty, argument, cultural references, and tone. Check that improved flow has not erased a distinctive choice or added an unsupported connection. Read for natural rhythm in this genre, not maximum lexical variety. When the original already works, leave it alone.

Return the edited text in the requested format. Add brief change notes only when useful or requested; follow copy-only instructions. For ambiguous editing choices, consult the [constructed examples](references/examples.md). Never claim the result is “undetectable,” definitively human, or scientifically proven to preserve voice.
