# Constructed editing examples

These are invented teaching examples, not quotations from research or user writing. Read them when choosing between a local repair and a broad stylistic rewrite.

## Remove padding without adding a claim

**Draft:** “In today's rapidly evolving landscape, our club serves as a vibrant hub that fosters meaningful connections. We meet in the library on Thursdays to swap books.”

**Possible edit:** “Our club meets in the library on Thursdays to swap books.”

**Why:** The second sentence supplies the actual activity. Do not invent attendance figures, friendships, or community impact to replace the empty description. Keep the warmer language if the user deliberately wants it and it serves the context.

## Preserve uncertainty and frustration

**Draft:** “I think the new form helped a bit. But we still had to enter every address twice, which was the bit that annoyed me.”

**Possible edit:** Leave it unchanged, or remove one “bit” if the user wants a light cleanup.

**Avoid:** “The redesigned form significantly improved efficiency, although redundant data entry remained a key challenge.”

**Why:** That rewrite invents confidence and replaces a person's ordinary irritation with an institutional summary.

## Keep the writer's language variety

**Draft:** “Please send it by Friday only; after that I'll be away for Onam.”

**Context:** The writer confirms that this regional usage and audience are intentional.

**Possible edit:** Leave it unchanged. If the audience needs clarification, offer “Please send it by Friday; after that I'll be away for Onam.”

**Why:** Cultural context belongs to the writer. Do not replace Onam with “the holidays” or insert an explanation the audience does not need. Do not infer nationality from the sentence.

## Keep technical conditions and consistent terms

**Draft:** “The cache may reduce latency when requests repeat. It does not reduce the cost of the first request.”

**Possible edit:** Leave it unchanged.

**Avoid:** “The cache slashes latency and dramatically lowers request costs.”

**Why:** The condition and limit are substantive. Repeating “request” is useful precision, not a diversity failure.

## Flag missing evidence

**Draft:** “Our workshops transformed participants' careers.”

**Possible response:** Ask what outcomes were measured, or suggest a supported narrower description such as “Our workshops covered interview practice and CV writing” **only if those activities are established in the supplied material**.

**Why:** A forceful specific claim is not automatically better than a vague one. Never invent a promotion story, testimonial, or percentage to make copy feel authentic.
